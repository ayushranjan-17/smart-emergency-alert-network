Motivation & Context

The Disaster Network Congestion Dilemma

Why standard Best-Effort FIFO routers fail during public emergencies

Civilian Traffic Surge

During earthquakes, floods, or industrial fires, civilian traffic surges by 300%–500% (social media, status updates, messaging, video).

Network edge routers rapidly fill their buffers, creating chronic congestion.

Critical Alert Starvation

Disaster response teams and evacuation broadcasts must traverse the exact same shared gateways.

In standard FIFO queueing, emergency alerts sit at the back of 50-packet queues, experiencing hundreds of milliseconds of latency or tail-drop losses.

Core Research Question: How can network-layer DiffServ QoS isolate and prioritize emergency alerts without over-provisioning expensive physical bandwidth?
Goal: Near-zero queuing delay + 100% packet delivery for life-critical dispatches.
Architecture

Network Topology & Overload Setup

Asymmetric star topology with a 40% overloaded bottleneck

  • Node 0 (Emergency Source): 200 Kbps, TOS 0x10
  • Node 1 (Ordinary 1): 600 Kbps, TOS 0x00
  • Node 2 (Ordinary 2): 600 Kbps, TOS 0x00
  • Node 3 (Gateway Router): Hosts Traffic Control QueueDisc
  • Node 4 (Response Centre): Listens on ports 9000, 9001, 9002
  • Access Links: 10 Mbps, 1 ms delay (Oversized)
  • Bottleneck Link: 1 Mbps, 10 ms delay

Implementation

End-to-End Priority Classification

From Application Type-of-Service to Multi-Band Router Dequeue

Step 1: Application

TOS Tagging

Emergency OnOffApplication sets Tos=0x10.

Ordinary applications set default Tos=0x00.

Step 2: Socket & L3

Priority Mapping

Socket::IpTos2Priority maps TOS 0x10 to Priority 6.

Gateway router preserves TOS byte upon IPv4 forwarding.

Step 3: Traffic Control

PrioQueueDisc

Priority 6 maps to Band 0 (High).

Priority 0 maps to Band 1 (Normal).

Band 0 dequeued first!

Hardware Queue Decoupling: The bottleneck NetDevice queue is set to 1p. This prevents packets from piling up in an unmanaged device hardware queue, ensuring all scheduling is governed by the Traffic Control layer.
Linux-compatible Traffic Control architecture in ns-3.49

CDF Analysis

Emergency Alert Delay CDF

Empirical cumulative distribution strictly bounded by queueing theory

Queueing Theory Proof: In Priority mode, 100% of emergency packets arrive between 15.77 ms (D_min) and 24.4 ms, bounded by D_min + residual of packet in transmission (mean 2.17 ms, max 4.34 ms) + 1 committed packet in device queue (4.34 ms), yielding mean 22.28 ms and max 24.4 ms. In FIFO mode, alerts suffer from queue bufferbloat (~232 ms) and phase-dependent packet loss.
